# Decision Graph

**An AI-native system for structured, updateable human decision-making**

## 1. Overview

Decision Graph is an AI-native product designed to help people make better decisions over time, not just answer one-off questions.

Most important decisions today are made in fragmented ways:
- in chats
- in notes
- in people’s heads
- under emotion, fatigue, urgency, and incomplete information

As a result, people often cannot clearly answer:
- What exactly am I deciding?
- What assumptions is this decision based on?
- What tradeoffs am I making?
- What changed since I made this decision?
- Should I update the decision now?
- What is the next decision that logically follows?

Decision Graph solves this by turning decisions into structured, evolving objects. Each decision becomes a node in a graph with context, constraints, options, assumptions, reasoning, outcomes, and links to future decisions.

It is not just an advisor. It is a persistent decision system.

---

## 2. The Problem

### The broken reality

People rarely fail only because they lack information. More often, they fail because decision-making itself is messy:
- important context is scattered
- assumptions remain implicit
- emotions distort tradeoffs
- prior decisions are forgotten
- new decisions are made without continuity
- changing conditions do not properly update prior logic

The result is decision fatigue, inconsistency, regret, and poor sequencing.

### Why existing tools fall short

Today’s alternatives are incomplete:
- **Notes apps** store thoughts but do not reason
- **Chatbots** respond but do not preserve durable decision structure
- **Task managers** track actions but not judgment
- **Spreadsheets** force structure but feel unnatural for human dilemmas
- **Friends and advisors** provide context, but inconsistently and without memory

The real gap is not advice.

The real gap is **decision continuity**.

## 3. Why This Matters

Human life and work are not isolated questions. They are sequences of linked decisions.

A hiring decision affects team structure.
A job decision affects housing, finances, relationships, and time.
A health decision affects energy, routine, and future constraints.
A founder decision affects capital, focus, morale, and strategy.

Yet most tools treat every decision as a fresh question.

That is the core mismatch:

> Human decisions are graph-shaped, but current tools are session-shaped.

People need support not only in making a decision, but in:
- defining the decision clearly
- structuring uncertainty
- surfacing tradeoffs
- making a call
- updating that call when reality changes
- using it to guide the next decision

That is the opportunity Decision Graph addresses.

## 5. What Decision Graph Is

Decision Graph is an AI system that helps users:
- express a decision in natural language
- convert it into a structured decision object
- identify missing information, assumptions, and hidden tradeoffs
- evaluate realistic options
- recommend the current best path
- store the reasoning behind the choice
- update the decision when new facts arrive
- generate the next logically connected decision

The result is a living graph of human judgment over time.

---

## 6. Core Concepts

### A. Decision Node

A decision node is a structured object representing one decision.

A node may include:
- decision question
- context
- objective
- deadline
- options
- constraints
- assumptions
- unknowns
- decision criteria
- criteria weighting
- evidence
- recommendation
- chosen path
- confidence level
- review trigger
- eventual outcome

### B. Decision Graph

A decision graph is the connected set of decisions across time.

Each new decision may:
- depend on an earlier one
- revise an earlier one
- be blocked by an earlier one
- be enabled by an earlier one
- inherit constraints from an earlier one

This allows the system to understand that decisions are not isolated events. They are linked states in an evolving chain.

### C. Decision Memory

Decision memory is the stored reasoning history of the user.

It remembers:
- what the user decided
- why they decided it
- what mattered most
- what assumptions were active
- what changed later
- which patterns led to better or worse outcomes

Decision memory turns AI from a session-based assistant into a continuity system.

### D. Belief Update

When new facts arrive, the system updates an existing decision instead of starting from scratch.

A user can say:
- salary changed
- new evidence came in
- timing changed
- an option disappeared
- an assumption failed

The system then revises the recommendation while showing exactly what changed and why.

### E. Next-Decision Recommendation Engine

Once one decision is made, the system proposes the next decision that logically follows.

Example:
- user chooses to move cities
- next decisions may include housing, commute, budget, role transition, or family planning

This is where the graph becomes active rather than archival.

## 8. Example Use Case

A manager says:

> “I think I need to fire someone on my team, but he is going through a financial crisis and I am conflicted.”

A typical assistant might jump straight to advice. Decision Graph should not.

Instead, it should:
- frame the actual decision
- separate facts from emotions
- identify business obligations and ethical concerns
- generate realistic options
- evaluate the tradeoffs
- recommend a path
- define review criteria
- store the reasoning

Then, when new information arrives later, it should continue from the same decision state.

This is fundamentally better than a one-off chat response because the system preserves the case, the reasoning, and the consequences.

## 11. Product Principles

### 1. Start from messy human input
The system should accept ambiguity. Users should not need to think in templates before they begin.

### 2. Make logic explicit
The system should surface tradeoffs, assumptions, and uncertainty instead of hiding them.

### 3. Preserve user agency
The product should support judgment, not replace it.

### 4. Treat decisions as evolving
A decision can be revisited, revised, or superseded.

### 5. Use past decisions as state
Each new decision should inherit relevant context from prior ones.

### 6. Learn from outcomes
The system should improve by observing which patterns led to regret, success, reversals, or confidence.
